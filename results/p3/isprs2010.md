## What the paper predicts

- Method (p. 288): banks digitised from a 1966 topographic map and Landsat/ASTER/ERS-2 images (1989-2009, resampled to 30 m), DSAS transects on a baseline that is a buffer of the 2009 bank, linear regression with the authors' inverse-squared-time weights (Tables 1-4, p. 289: the last image carries 0.32-0.43 of the weight for 2015), extrapolated to 2015 and 2020. Base year 2009: last image 24 Jun 2009 at Sa Đéc (wet season), 30 Jan 2009 at sites 2-4. No interval and no hindcast; the only skill figure is a correlation coefficient per transect.
- Printed numbers: the predicted change per transect for 2015 and 2020 in Appendices 1-7 (p. 290-292; m, negative = erosion) and headline numbers in the text. The change is read here as the predicted bank minus the 2009 bank (the paper does not say whether the observed or the regressed 2009 position is meant).

## Site matching

Each site-side was traced on the paper's map (Figs. 3-6, p. 289-290; UTM grid ticks on the frame) as 3-6 vertices of the 2009 bank, taken as Indian 1960 / UTM 48N (EPSG:3148, the datum the paper names), moved to WGS84 (shift ~10 m E, ~90 m N). Against our 2009 composite (data/raw/*/mndwi_2009.tif) the traces are off by 70-460 m in directions that differ between maps, so the match is along the bank: start at our transect-side (named channel and side) whose 2008-10 bank is nearest to the trace's anchor (cape apex or islet head, a shape both data sets show; the upstream end at Sa Đéc) and keep our transects for the trace length along the bank (measured on our running-median 2008-10 bank; the same walk along our transect origins, i.e. centreline or island ring, gives the alternative extent used for the range). Our transects are ~100 m apart, as are theirs (stated for Sa Đéc). - Our 2008-10 banks follow the water edge of our 2009 composite; the paper's traced 2009 banks lie 70-460 m from them (median per site-side in the table), e.g. the Long Khánh A head ~400 m east of ours and the Châu Ma west face ~450 m east of ours. Likely causes: the paper's georeferencing (to a 1966 1:50,000 map, Indian 1960), a digitised soil / vegetation edge against our first water from land (sand spits at island heads in Jan-Apr), and our reading of the traces. The site ends are therefore uncertain by a few hundred metres along the bank, which matters most for the maximum. Read each match as a candidate stretch, not a transect-by-transect pairing; LK-5 and CM-6 keep only 8 and 9 measured transects (non-detections and edge switches at the islet heads).

## Reading

- Mainland banks, over-predicted. Sa Đéc: 100 m measured against 252 m predicted by 2020 (median, ratio 0.40; max 269 vs 527 m). Our 2001 -> 2009 rate there (20 m/yr) is close to the paper's implied 22 m/yr, so the history behind the forecast was right; retreat went on to ~2015 (90 m) and then nearly stopped (10 m more by 2020; cause not examined here). Thường Phước left side: 118 m predicted, -1 m measured (our 2001 -> 2009 rate was already only 5 m/yr). Thường Phước right side: 103 m of accretion predicted, 11 m measured; the 2001 -> 2009 accretion (43 m/yr in our data; the paper notes strong accretion in 2003-2009) did not continue.
- Islet heads, mostly under-predicted. Long Khánh A (both faces) and the Châu Ma east face kept eroding at 15-16 m/yr (Theil-Sen, median): measured 2020 medians 171-198 m against 71-142 m predicted (ratios 1.20-2.50); the weighted fit had implied 5-14 m/yr. The Châu Ma west face (CM-6) is undecided: 59 or 130 m measured against 123 m predicted, depending on the matched extent.
- Matching range: with the alternative extent the 2020 medians are LK-5 210, CM-6 130 m (other site-sides within 10 m of the primary). The sign of the miss changes only for CM-6.
- Plain reading: the weighted linear extrapolation missed in both directions, by 29-152 m at the median at an 11-yr horizon. It over-predicted where erosion slowed or stopped after 2009 (mainland bends) and under-predicted where it went on (most islet-head faces), and it gave no interval that could have warned of either. This argues for forecasts with verified coverage rather than for a better point rate.
- Limits: one paper, seven site-sides, descriptive only. The matches are candidate stretches (map georeferencing off by up to ~460 m); the maxima depend on the site ends and on edge switches at islet heads. The paper's base is its own digitised 2009 bank (wet-season image at Sa Đéc), ours a dry-season 3-yr mean 2008-10; a base offset of a few tens of metres does not change the reading.
