## Q1. Full content of the 2025 Physical Geography paper, Binh et al. 2024 (Ham Luong), and other 2023–2026 Tien/Hau ML susceptibility or forecasting papers

### Takeaway
Only two ML erosion-susceptibility papers cover the VMD: Nguyen T.G. et al. 2025 (Physical Geography, delta-wide, labels are Landsat-detected erosion points, best model GB AUC 0.85) and Binh et al. 2024 (Ham Luong, logistic regression, 100 points, AUC 0.86). Neither forecasts bank retreat in time, and neither validates against an independent geocoded inventory of reported sites. Two other papers matter more as same-region competitors: Bui et al. 2026 (Russian Geology and Geophysics) works with **255 recorded erosion sites on the Tien and Hau** (clustered with SOM), and Huy et al. 2026 (Environ. Res. Commun.) covers multi-decadal bankline change in the Vam Nao channel (An Giang).

### Cited Findings
**Nguyen T.G. et al. 2025, Physical Geography** [V-Crossref]
- Full reference: Tien Giang Nguyen, Dinh Kha Dang, Chi Tuan Ngo, Huu Duy Nguyen, Viet Thanh Pham, Quang-Thanh Bui (all University of Science, VNU Hanoi), Thu-Van Can (HCMC University of Natural Resources and Environment) and Ninh Van Luu (VNU University of Science, plus **Hydro-Meteorology Center An Giang Province**). Published online 12 Sep 2025. doi:10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849 — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849)
- Labels: erosion points were detected from Landsat OLI images of 2010, 2015 and 2024. Models used **16 conditioning factors** in four groups: topography, climate, hydrology and human activities. — [T&F abstract, V-snippet](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849)
- Models and validation: AdaBoost, CatBoost, Gradient Boosting and Random Forest. AUC values were GB 0.85, ADB 0.84, RF 0.83 and CB 0.82. — [T&F abstract, V-snippet](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849)
- Results: erosion is most serious in **An Giang and Dong Thap, along the main Tien and Hau branches**. The high-to-very-high susceptibility class covers about 1,337 km² under RF and about 1,272 km² under GB. One search summary said the range across models was "~600 to 1,337 km²". — [T&F abstract, V-snippet](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849)
- No temporal forecast is mentioned in the abstract. The output is a static susceptibility map. Semantic Scholar showed 0 citations on 21 Oct 2026, the abstract is elided by the publisher, and there is no open-access PDF. — [Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849?fields=title,citationCount,openAccessPdf)

**Binh, Quyet & Tuan 2024, VNU J. Sci. EES (Ham Luong River)** [V-Crossref][V-page]
- Doan Van Binh, Le Van Quyet, Luc Anh Tuan. "Predict the Riverbank Erosion Susceptibility for the Ham Luong River Using the Logistic Regression Model." *VNU Journal of Science: Earth and Environmental Sciences* 40(4), 25 Dec 2024. doi:10.25073/2588-1094/vnuees.5234 — [article page](https://js.vnu.edu.vn/EES/article/view/5234)
- Method: logistic regression on **100 riverbank locations labelled eroded or stable**, with 12 conditioning variables. 15-fold CV gave ACC 0.83 and AUC 0.86. - Scope note: the Ham Luong is a Tien River distributary in Ben Tre, not the An Giang/Dong Thap main stem.

### Inferences
- The planned paper's claim (3), that existing susceptibility maps are validated on reported or detected points, holds for both ML papers. The Physical Geography paper uses Landsat-detected points. Binh 2024 uses 100 labelled points of unstated origin. Neither tests against an independent, geocoded reported-site inventory or a later time window.
- The closest same-region competitor for the inventory is the Bui/Tran group at Hanoi University of Mining and Geology. Bui & Tran 2023 cite 248 investigated locations since 2015, and Bui et al. 2026 cite 255 recorded sites on the Tien and Hau. These sites must exist internally as point records with attributes such as site length and distances to faults, roads and buildings, because SOM needs them. They have not been published as an open dataset as far as I could find. The paper should cite these works and distinguish its own inventory: public release, geocoding and provenance, per-year dates, and main-channel scope.
- The Physical Geography co-author at the An Giang Hydro-Meteorology Center suggests access to provincial incident records. That group could produce the next paper in the planned focus region.

## Q2. Does any geocoded point inventory of erosion sites exist, and is any downloadable?

### Takeaway
A geocoded inventory of serious sites exists: the VDDMA/MARD WebGIS "Bản đồ sạt lở bờ sông, xói lở bờ biển vùng ĐBSCL" launched in June 2018 with 562 sites. Point records also exist inside papers (Bui et al. 2026: 255 sites). However, I found **no downloadable, point-level public dataset** of individual erosion sites for the Tien/Hau. The only open GIS download (Open Development Mekong, Dong Thap 2016–2019) is administrative-unit aggregated. The 2018 WebGIS subdomain did not respond on 21 Oct 2026.

## Q3. Any forecasting of Tien/Hau bank retreat after 2010, and was uncertainty given?

### Takeaway
No calibrated or probabilistic bank-retreat forecast for the Tien/Hau was found. Two corrections to the planned claim (2). First, the 2010 ISPRS paper was followed by a 2011 journal version (J. Resources & Ecology) from the same group using the same DSAS approach. Second, SIWRR published a 2024 TELEMAC-2D/SISYPHE delta-wide **10-year morphological projection** (Tien: "mean erosion 10 m in 10 years"). It is mainly bed/channel change, and no uncertainty was reported. A 2026 Science Advances counterfactual model quantifies mining-driven incision and bank widening (2017–2021) but is not a forward forecast.

### Cited Findings
- **ISPRS 2010:** Lam Dao Nguyen, Nguyen Thanh Minh, Pham Thi Mai Thy, Hoang Phi Phung, Hoang Van Huan. "Analysis of changes in the riverbanks of Mekong River – Vietnam by using multi-temporal remote sensing data." *Int. Arch. Photogramm. Remote Sens. Spatial Inf. Sci.* XXXVIII Part 8, Kyoto 2010. Data are Landsat MSS/TM/ETM+, ASTER and ERS-2 (1989–2009) plus 1966–68 topographic maps. Method: **DSAS linear regression with an author-designed weighting (Matlab) that favours recent years**, predicting 2015 and 2020. Sa Dec hot spot erosion was up to 50 m/yr, with a forecast of 100–520 m in 10 years. At Thuong Phuoc (left side) the forecast was max 184 m by 2015 and 330 m by 2020, with the right side accreting 108 m and 171 m. Long Khanh A islet head: 169 m by 2015 and 254 m by 2020. Chau Ma (Hong Ngu): 100 m by 2015 and 200 m by 2020. The only skill statistic is "correlation coefficients >80% in almost all transects". **No confidence intervals or hindcast validation.** — [ISPRS PDF, V-ISPRS](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVIII/part8/pdf/W01OH3_20100308201836.pdf)
- **J. Resources & Ecology 2011:** Nguyen Lam-Dao, Viet Pham-Bach, Minh Nguyen-Thanh, Mai-Thy Pham-Thi, Phung Hoang-Phi. "Change Detection of Land use and Riverbank in Mekong Delta, Vietnam using Time Series Remotely Sensed Data." *J. Resour. Ecol.* 2(4): 370–374. doi:10.3969/j.issn.1674-764x.2011.04.011. Uses DSAS "to analyze trend of changes in terms of changing prediction". The Tien and Hau left and right banks are hot spots, with 1989–2009 imagery. The abstract gives no prediction years and no uncertainty. — [BioOne page, V-page](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Resources-and-Ecology/volume-2/issue-4/j.issn.1674-764x.2011.04.011/Change-Detection-of-Land-use-and-Riverbank-in-Mekong-Delta/10.3969/j.issn.1674-764x.2011.04.011.full). The DOI did not resolve in Crossref, so it is likely registered elsewhere.
- **SIWRR 2024 (Vietnamese):** Trần Tuấn Anh, Nguyễn Bình Dương, Lê Mạnh Hùng, Nguyễn Nghĩa Hùng (Viện Khoa học Thủy lợi miền Nam). ## Q4. Key physical-driver studies (one line each)

### Takeaway
The causal chain in the literature runs: dam sediment trapping plus sand mining greatly exceeding supply, leading to bed incision (~0.1–0.5 m/yr on the Tien), steeper and higher banks, and bank failure. Hackney 2020 makes the bank-instability link explicit for the lower Mekong (Cambodia focus). Binh 2021 and Kumar 2026 quantify incision in the VMD.

### Cited Findings
- **Hackney C.R. et al. 2020**, *Nature Sustainability* 3: 217–225, doi:10.1038/s41893-019-0455-3. Sand flux into the delta is about 6.18 Mt/yr (bedload about 0.18 Mt/yr) against about 50 Mt/yr extracted. Bed lowering at these rates destabilises banks. The press release says the work focused on the Cambodian Mekong. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41893-019-0455-3); [Exeter ORE, V-snippet](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/40127); [Southampton press release, V-snippet](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/news/2020/01/mekong-river-sand.page)
- **Binh D.V., Kantoush S.A., Sumi T. et al. 2021**, *Hydrological Processes* 35(2): e14030, doi:10.1002/hyp.14030. Riverbed incision upstream of My Thuan accelerated from −0.16 m/yr (−16.7 Mm³/yr, 1998–2014) to −0.5 m/yr (−52.5 Mm³/yr, 2014–2017). Dry-season water level at My Thuan dropped by up to 46% despite a 23% increase in discharge. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/hyp.14030)
- **Kumar S. et al. 2026**, *Science Advances* 12(28): eaef0558, doi:10.1126/sciadv.aef0558. A counterfactual (no-mining) morphodynamic model attributes about 25–30% of total incision to mining (about 0.10 m/yr across about 65% of channels) and reports bank widening up to 15 m (press release). — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/sciadv.aef0558); [NTU, V-page](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/nie/news-events/news/detail/sand-mining-is-destabilising-the-mekong-delta-far-more-than-previously-understood--nie--ntu-singapore-study-finds)
- **Jordan C. et al. 2019**, *Scientific Reports* 9: 17823, doi:10.1038/s41598-019-53804-z. Repeat bathymetry in 2018 on about 20 km of the Tien gives 4.64 ± 0.31 Mm³/yr extracted. Delta-wide reported statistics (17.77 Mm³/yr) are likely too conservative, and upstream supply cannot compensate. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41598-019-53804-z)
- **Anthony E.J. et al. 2015**, *Scientific Reports* 5: 14745, doi:10.1038/srep14745. SPOT-5 imagery for 2003–2012 shows that more than 50% of the more than 600 km **coastal** shoreline is eroding. The authors link this to dam sediment retention, channel sand mining and groundwater-driven subsidence. It is a coastline study, so cite it for driver context only. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/srep14745)
- **Yuen K.W., Park E. et al. 2024**, *Communications Earth & Environment* 5: 31, doi:10.1038/s43247-023-01161-1. Illegal mining in the VMD was 16.7 Mm³/yr in 2013 and 15.5 Mm³/yr in 2018–2020. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s43247-023-01161-1)
- **Park E. 2024**, *Sci. Total Environ.* 924: 171620, doi:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2024.171620, "Sand mining in the Mekong Delta: Extent and compounded impacts". NTU reports about 53 Mm³ extracted in 2022, versus about 20 Mm³ declared (V-snippet via NTU news). — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2024.171620)
- **Ahmed M.F. et al. 2025**, *Geomorphology* 470: 109535. Spatiotemporal susceptibility mapping of riverbed incision under sand mining. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.geomorph.2024.109535)
- **Darby S.E. et al. 2013**, *WRR* 49: 2146–2163. Hydroclimatic drivers of Mekong bank erosion (IOD, tropical storms, snowmelt). — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/wrcr.20205)
- **Tran D.D. et al. 2023**, *Scientific Reports* 13: 15937, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-43114-w. A Bassac resident survey found low awareness of the mining–bank-erosion link. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41598-023-43114-w)
- **Tri V.P.D. et al. 2022**, *Int. J. River Basin Manag.* 21(3): 501–512, doi:10.1080/15715124.2021.2021926. Social vulnerability to riverbank erosion across the VMD. — [Crossref, V-Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/15715124.2021.2021926)
- **Nga T.N.Q., Bay N.T., Long T.T., Hoai H.C. 2025.** "Drivers and mechanisms of erosion in the Vietnamese Mekong Delta." In *The Mekong Delta Environmental Research Guidebook* (Elsevier), pp. 107–130, doi:10.1016/b978-0-443-23673-0.00005-2. Hackney C.R. 2024, "Morphological changes in the Mekong Delta and their implications for livelihoods", in *The Mekong River Basin* (Elsevier), doi:10.1016/b978-0-323-90814-6.00008-5. These are review chapters. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/b978-0-443-23673-0.00005-2)

### Inferences
- For the paper's driver covariates, the defensible set is local incision or bathymetric change (Binh 2021; Kumar 2026; SIWRR 2024), mining-zone proximity (131 zones in Kumar 2026; Jordan 2019), bank slope and height (Binh 2024; Dang & Tran 2026), and, as a newer, contested factor, distance to active faults (Bui et al. 2026).
