## Q1. Has any study compared reported/inventory-based erosion labels with remote-sensing-derived bank-retreat labels for training or validation, or quantified AUC inflation from label or sampling bias for riverbank, streambank, gully, coastal or flood susceptibility?

### Takeaway
The searches above found no riverbank, streambank, gully or coastal study that scores one susceptibility model against both a reported inventory and a satellite-derived bank-retreat inventory, or that measures an AUC gap as a function of distance to roads or settlements. The flood literature names the bias, and one flood study compares historical and SAR-expanded inventories, but none of those studies quantifies AUC inflation from reporting bias. The general mechanism (report-based inventories cluster near infrastructure and inflate or distort validation) is well established in landslide science (Q2).

### Inferences
- The concept "report-based labels cluster near infrastructure and give misleadingly high AUC" is **not novel**. Steger 2016/2017/2021, Stanley & Kirschbaum 2017, Bornaetxea 2018/2022/2023 and Lima 2021 cover it for landslides, and Phillips 2009 and Hijmans 2012 cover it for SDM. Reviewers in NHESS, Geomorphology or ESPL will know this literature, and the paper must cite it as the origin.
- The **design** (one model scored against two label provenances on the same terrain) has precedent: Bornaetxea 2023 for landslides and, loosely, Yu 2023 for floods. A proximity-conditioned AUC gap (inflation as a function of distance to roads or settlements) was not found in any field in these searches.
- The **domain transfer** (fluvial bank erosion, delta rivers, satellite bank-retreat labels as the reference) was not found.

## Q2. In the landslide literature (Steger 2016, 2017; Stanley & Kirschbaum 2017; Huang 2024+; inventory incompleteness, presence-only sampling bias, road or accessibility bias), has the method been transferred to fluvial/riverbank erosion or flood/gully susceptibility, and which bias-correction or bias-quantification techniques are used?

### Takeaway
The landslide toolbox is mature: bias-describing covariates zeroed at prediction, mixed-effects random intercepts for areas mapped with uneven completeness, restricting training to the effectively surveyed or visible area, training near roads only, random effects for road density, and slope units against positional error. No transfer of these techniques to riverbank erosion was found. For gully and flood work, "distance to road" is routinely used as an ordinary predictor, with no bias framing.

### Cited Findings
- **Steger, Brenning, Bell, Glade (2016a).** The propagation of inventory-based positional errors into statistical landslide susceptibility models. *NHESS* 16:2729. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-16-2729-2016 [V-CR, V-WEB abstract]. Larger positional errors cause "increasing distortions of modelling and validation results". This "can be partly counteracted by adapting the modelling design".
- **Steger, Brenning, Bell, Petschko, Glade (2016b).** Exploring discrepancies between quantitative validation results and the geomorphic plausibility of statistical landslide susceptibility maps. *Geomorphology* 262. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomorph.2016.03.015 [V-CR; content UNVERIFIED-CONTENT]. Cited via Stanley & Kirschbaum (2017) for the point that bias can improve validation statistics ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170011349/downloads/20170011349.pdf)).
- **Steger, Brenning, Bell, Glade (2017).** The influence of systematically incomplete shallow landslide inventories on statistical susceptibility models and suggestions for improvements. *Landslides* 14:1767–1781. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10346-017-0820-0 [V-CR; findings via search summary, SNIPPET]. They simulated incompleteness by removing landslides in forests and in selected municipalities. **The highest predictive performance came from models on highly incomplete inventories that included predictors describing the incompleteness, and those models gave the most erroneous maps.** Dropping those predictors confounded the remaining relationships. Mixed-effects models were least affected. They propose a four-step procedure. The univie record is [here](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/de/publications/the-influence-of-systematically-incomplete-shallow-landslide-inve/). A related EGU2018 abstract recommends rejecting predictors tied to incompleteness "even if estimated predictive performances ... might suggest the opposite" ([EGU2018-8551](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-8551.pdf)) [SNIPPET].
- **Steger et al. (2021)**, see Q1 #1. Three model designs: M1 ignores data-collection effects; M2 zeroes them (distance to infrastructure and similar covariates are not used at prediction); M3 is an "intervention index" that keeps the bias and serves as a damage-oriented map, with prediction rate 0.95 on 64 storm-event landslides ([EGU2020-3440](https://presentations.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-3440_presentation.pdf)) [V-WEB].
- **Lima, Steger, Glade (2021)**, see Q1 #4. Mixed-effects logistic regression handles heterogeneous completeness, and slope units handle positional inaccuracy [V-CR].
- **Bornaetxea, Rossi, Marchesini, Alvioli (2018).** Effective surveyed area and its role in statistical landslide susceptibility assessments. *NHESS* 18:2455. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-18-2455-2018 [V-CR, abstract read]. Train only within the "effective surveyed area" delineated from field-survey paths, because unsurveyed cells wrongly get absence labels. Slope units reduce the uncertainty further.
- **Bornaetxea et al. (2022)**, see Q1 #7. Classify terrain by visibility from roads and compare inventory density across visibility classes [V-CR].
- **Stanley & Kirschbaum (2017)**, see Q1 #2. Train within 1 km of highways and validate in remote areas [V-WEB].
- **Felsberg, Poesen, Bechtold, Vanmaercke, De Lannoy (2022).** Estimating global landslide susceptibility and its uncertainty through ensemble modeling. *NHESS* 22:3063. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-22-3063-2022 [V-CR]. Per a snippet, road-network density is treated as a random effect "to mitigate potential landslide inventory bias" [SNIPPET].
- **He & Zhang (2022).** Comparison of three mixed-effects models for mass movement susceptibility mapping based on incomplete inventory in China. *Remote Sens.* 14:6068. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14236068 [V-CR, abstract read]. GLMM, GAMM and tree-boosted mixed-effects models, with land use and geological division as random intercepts for inventory bias. The models were tested on a "simulated highly biased inventory".
- **Moreno, Lombardo, Crespi, Zellner, Mair, Pittore, ... (2024).** Space-time data-driven modeling of precipitation-induced shallow landslides in South Tyrol, Italy. *Sci. Total Environ.* https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.169166 [V-CR]. Per a search summary, the GAMMs include adjustments for spatial and temporal bias in the landslide data [SNIPPET].
- **Huang, Mao, Jiang, Zhou, Fan, Zeng, Catani, Yu (2024).** Uncertainties in landslide susceptibility prediction modeling: A review on the incompleteness of landslide inventory and its influence rules. *Geosci. Front.* 15(6):101886. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gsf.2024.101886 [V-CR; content UNVERIFIED-CONTENT]. A companion paper by Huang et al. (2024) in *J. Rock Mech. Geotech. Eng.* 16(10):4177–4191 on positional-error levels reports that RF and MLP accuracy falls with positional error and that semi-supervised ML helps [SNIPPET; DOI not checked].
- **Qi & Xia (2026).** Inventory incompleteness-induced uncertainty in machine learning-based landslide susceptibility modeling: A comparative and interpretable analysis. *Appl. Sci.* 16(15):7587. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16157587 [V-CR; content not read].
- **Khan, Kainthola, Bahuguna, Ahmed, Abioui (2025).** Unravelling the impact of landslide inventory on landslide susceptibility in the Indian Himalaya. *Phys. Chem. Earth* 103930. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2025.103930 [V-CR title only; content not read].
- A road effect can also be real, not only a reporting artefact. Landslide odds near highways in the southern Ecuadorian Andes were about an order of magnitude higher, fading at around 150 m, after confounder adjustment ([NHESS 15:45, 2015](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/15/45/2015/nhess-15-45-2015.pdf)) [SNIPPET; DOI not Crossref-checked]. Stanley & Kirschbaum (2017) likewise say part of the road clustering is "probably due to anthropogenic disturbance" [V-WEB].
- Positive-unlabeled (PU) learning has been used to choose non-landslide samples (PU bagging compared with buffer and K-means sampling). No PU application to bank erosion was found ([search result: Sci. Rep. 2024 non-landslide sampling](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10965908/)) [SNIPPET; whether this specific paper uses PU is unconfirmed].
- In gully susceptibility, distance to road is a routine predictor with inconsistent sign. For example, frequency ratio is above 1 within 198 m of roads on the Loess Plateau ([T&F 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19475705.2024.2421382)) [SNIPPET]. No gully paper found treats road proximity as a sampling-bias variable.

### Inferences
- Methods the planned paper can borrow, and must cite:
  1. Treat distance to roads and settlements as a bias-describing covariate. Report AUC with and without it, and with it zeroed at prediction (Steger 2017/2021).
  2. Use random intercepts per province or district for uneven reporting completeness (Steger 2017; Lima 2021; He & Zhang 2022).
  3. Restrict training to an effective surveyed or visible area (Bornaetxea 2018/2022).
  4. Train near roads and validate far from them (Stanley & Kirschbaum 2017).
  5. Use target-group background and spatial filtering or thinning from SDM (Q3).
- A key caveat from this literature is that a road or settlement effect on riverbank erosion can be physical (bank loading by houses, roads and embankments; boat waves; sand-mining access). The planned paper must separate the physical and reporting components, for example by checking whether satellite-label erosion rates also rise near settlements. Without that, a reviewer will say the "inflation" is partly real susceptibility.
- Transfer to riverbank erosion was not found in any of the queries listed. Transfer to flood work is limited to naming the bias (Sen & Das 2026) and to augmenting inventories (Yu 2023).

### Gaps
- The full text of Huang et al. (2024, GSF) was not read. It may catalogue an application outside landslides; unconfirmed.
- No EGU 2024–2026 abstract on erosion, gully or flood "inventory bias" was found by these queries. EGU meeting-organizer search was not run directly.

## Q3. In species distribution modelling (sampling bias, target-group background, Phillips et al. 2009), has anyone ported this to hazard maps of bank erosion?

### Takeaway
SDM has the canonical theory, and evidence of AUC inflation, for spatially biased presence-only data. No port to bank-erosion hazard maps was found. A search for target-group background in landslide or flood susceptibility returned no hazard application.

### Cited Findings
- **Phillips, Dudík, Elith, Graham, Lehmann, Leathwick, Ferrier (2009).** Sample selection bias and presence-only distribution models: implications for background and pseudo-absence data. *Ecol. Appl.* 19:181. https://doi.org/10.1890/07-2153.1 [V-CR, abstract read]. Occurrence collection is "spatially biased toward easily accessed areas". Proposes background data "with the same bias as occurrence data", meaning target-group background. Across 226 species it improved performance for all methods tested, with gains largest under strong bias.
- **Hijmans (2012)**, see Q1 #9. Spatial sorting bias inflates AUC. Proposes pairwise-distance sampling and a null-model-calibrated AUC (cAUC) [V-CR]. This is directly usable in the planned paper as a control.
- **Kramer-Schadt et al. (2013).** The importance of correcting for sampling bias in MaxEnt species distribution models. *Divers. Distrib.* https://doi.org/10.1111/ddi.12096 [V-CR]. Compares spatial filtering of occurrences with a background biased like the sample.
- **Fourcade, Engler, Rödder, Secondi (2014).** Mapping species distributions with MAXENT using a geographically biased sample of presence data: a performance assessment of methods for correcting sampling bias. *PLoS ONE* 9:e97122. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0097122 [V-CR; content not read].
- A 2026 arXiv preprint on Himalayan GLOF and landslide sites warns that ranking failed sites against an "un-matched background pool" overstates signal and calls for matched controls ([arXiv 2608.12422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12422)) [SNIPPET]. This is the closest hazard-side echo of the SDM argument.

### Inferences
- Hijmans' cAUC and pairwise-distance sampling address the main confound the planned paper must rule out. Reported sites are spatially clustered, so random-split AUC is inflated by spatial sorting alone, whether or not road bias is present. The "reported vs satellite" AUC gap must be computed under spatial or pairwise-distance validation so that road-proximity bias is separated from clustering.
- "First port of target-group background or SDM bias correction to riverbank-erosion susceptibility" could be a secondary novelty claim. The only evidence is absence from the queries above, so word it as "to our knowledge".

### Gaps
- Phillips 2009 and Hijmans 2012 are heavily cited, but citing-paper lists in Scopus or Google Scholar were not crawled. A citing paper in geomorphology or hazards could exist undetected.

## Q4. Does any Mekong, Vietnam, Bangladesh or India riverbank-susceptibility study discuss or test label bias?

### Takeaway
The closest direct competitor, a 2025 Physical Geography paper on Tien and Hau riverbank-erosion susceptibility in the Vietnamese Mekong Delta, appears (per search summaries) to use **Landsat-detected erosion points** as labels, with AUC around 0.82–0.85. No discussion of label bias was found in the material available. Other Vietnam, Bangladesh and India studies either use satellite-derived bank-line change, field indices or unspecified site sets, and none found tests label bias. Vietnam does have a large official reported-site list (around 740 erosion sites), which is the "reported label" source the claim relies on.

### Cited Findings
- **Nguyen, Dang, Ngo, Nguyen, Pham, Bui, Can, Van Luu (2025).** Prediction of riverbank erosion susceptibility areas of the Vietnam Mekong Delta using machine learning and remote sensing in the context of geographical studies. *Physical Geography* (vol. 47 no. 2 per T&F listing). https://doi.org/10.1080/02723646.2025.2555849 [V-CR; full text and abstract blocked (HTTP 403); details SNIPPET]. Search summaries say it uses Landsat OLI 2010/2015/2024 to detect erosion points and 16 conditioning factors, including human activities. AUC: GB 0.85, AdaBoost 0.84, RF 0.83, CatBoost 0.82. Hotspots are in An Giang and Dong Thap along the Tien and Hau. **This is the main competitor on study area. Its label source (satellite) is the opposite of the "reported labels" premise.**
- **Doan Van Binh, Le Van Quyet, Luc Anh Tuan (2024).** Predict the riverbank erosion susceptibility for the Ham Luong River using the logistic regression model. *VNU J. Sci.: Earth Environ. Sci.* 40(4). https://js.vnu.edu.vn/EES/article/view/5234 [V-WEB]. 100 locations labelled eroded or stable, 12 variables, 15-fold CV. The page does not say where the labels came from and does not discuss sampling bias.
- **Duy, Ty, Phat, Minh, Thanh, Downes (2025).** Assessing river corridor stability and erosion dynamics in the Mekong Delta. *Earth* 6(2):34. https://doi.org/10.3390/earth6020034 [V-CR, abstract read]. Landsat 2000–2023, field surveys and numerical simulation. 88% of banks in Dong Thap and 48% in An Giang do not comply with conservation standards. No susceptibility AUC and no label-bias test.
- **Pham (2024).** An overview of riverbank erosion prediction techniques applied to the Mekong Delta, Vietnam. *E3S Web Conf.* 535:01020. https://doi.org/10.1051/e3sconf/202453501020 [V-CR, abstract read]. Per SIWRR as reported in Vietnamese press, the Mekong Delta has 743 erosion sites, about 690 of them riverbank sites and 168 especially dangerous ([Tuổi Trẻ/Saigon Times](https://tuoitre.vn/saigontimes/dong-bang-song-cuu-long-co-hon-740-diem-sat-lo-1061012615.htm)) [SNIPPET; page not fetched; date not confirmed].

Satellite hotspots combined with exposure. Notes that water-level sensitivity can cause inaccurate bank-line detection [SNIPPET]. No label-bias test.
- A Cambodian Mekong vulnerability study used snowball sampling seeded by commune heads (Heliyon 2024, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e25418; [PMC10847654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10847654/)) [SNIPPET]. This is a social-survey selection bias, not a susceptibility-label bias.
- India and Bangladesh: no label-bias test found. Examples are Middle Brahmaputra (Assam) vulnerability with ANN-MLP and RF ([Catena 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0341816223006720)) [SNIPPET]; Ganga (Malda) binary logistic regression with 9 factors ([Model. ### Inferences
- **Straw-man risk.** The nearest Mekong competitor (Nguyen et al. 2025) already trains and validates on satellite-derived erosion points. A reviewer may answer that VMD susceptibility maps are not validated against reported sites. The planned paper should document actual practice: which provincial, MONRE, SIWRR or VDDMA products or studies use the reported-site list for zoning or validation. It could also reframe the claim as "what if official reported-site inventories are used", since those lists exist and drive policy (around 740 sites).
- **Opportunity.** Nobody in the Mekong, Bangladesh or India literature found here compares the two label provenances. This regional and fluvial gap is the strongest surviving part of the claim.

### Gaps
- The full text of Nguyen et al. (2025) could not be read (HTTP 403), so its label source and any bias discussion are confirmed only through search summaries. **This must be checked by hand before submission.**
- Vietnamese provincial reports and VDDMA zoning methods were not reviewed.

## Overall verdict: which part of the novelty claim survives?

### Takeaway
**Killed (not novel):** the general proposition that reported or inventory labels cluster near roads and settlements and so inflate or distort susceptibility validation AUC. It is established for landslides (Steger 2016/2017/2021, Stanley & Kirschbaum 2017, Bornaetxea 2018/2022/2023, Lima 2021) and for SDM (Phillips 2009, Hijmans 2012). **Partly pre-empted:** the "same model, different label provenance" design (Bornaetxea 2023 for landslides; Yu 2023 for floods). **Survives, narrowed:** (a) the first paired quantification for **fluvial or riverbank erosion**, scoring one susceptibility model against official reported erosion sites and against satellite-derived bank-retreat labels on the same reaches; (b) expressing the AUC gap **as a function of distance to settlements and roads** (proximity-stratified or conditional AUC), which was not found in any field in these searches; (c) testing the landslide and SDM bias corrections (zeroed bias covariate, mixed effects, target-group background, effective surveyed area, cAUC) on bank erosion.

### Cited Findings
- Steger et al. (2021) give a concrete precedent for the size of the effect: CV-AUROC 0.87 with data-collection effects included versus 0.59 with them zeroed ([EGU2020-3440](https://presentations.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-3440_presentation.pdf)). The planned paper's inflation estimate should be compared with this.
- Image-derived inventories have their own road-related bias (under-representation of small features near roads) ([Bornaetxea et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-22-2929-2022)). Landsat detection limits also apply ([Langhorst & Pavelsky 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JF006774)). The satellite labels are therefore a *different-bias* reference, not ground truth.
- Clustered presences inflate AUC through spatial sorting alone ([Hijmans 2012](https://doi.org/10.1890/11-0826.1)).

### Inferences
- Suggested reframed claim: "the first quantification, for riverbank erosion, of how label provenance (official reported sites vs satellite bank-retreat) changes susceptibility-model performance and spatial pattern, with the discrepancy resolved by distance to settlements and roads, and a test of landslide- and SDM-derived bias corrections". Say "label-source sensitivity" or "apparent AUC inflation", not "inflation relative to truth".
- Must-do controls so reviewers cannot collapse the result into known effects:
  1. Use spatial or pairwise-distance (cAUC) validation, to separate clustering from road bias.
  2. Show whether satellite-label erosion rates also rise near settlements, to separate physical from reporting effects.
  3. Characterise the satellite labels' detection limit, which is a function of bank-retreat magnitude and Landsat or Sentinel resolution.
- Cite Steger 2017/2021, Stanley & Kirschbaum 2017, Bornaetxea 2018/2022/2023, Lima 2021, Phillips 2009 and Hijmans 2012 as the conceptual origin. Omitting them would be the most likely reason for rejection.
